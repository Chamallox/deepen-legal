Readloop Privacy Policy

Last updated: 11 August 2026

Readloop is a reading app. You log the pages you have read, the app asks you one open question, you answer it out loud, and your answer becomes concepts on a map that links everything you read.

This page describes exactly what that involves. It is written against the app's actual behaviour rather than as a general statement of intent.

There is no account

Readloop has no sign-up, no login, no email address and no password. Nothing you do in the app is tied to a name, an email address, or a social profile, because the app never asks for any of them.

Your books, your notes, your recordings, your word cards and your saved passages are stored on your device. They are not backed up to a Readloop server, and there is no server-side copy of your library that we or anyone else could look at.

What leaves your device, and where it goes

Four things leave your device. Each one is listed with what it contains and who receives it.

1. Your recordings and notes, to turn speech into text

When you record a recap, the audio file is sent to Readloop's processing service, which passes it to OpenAI for transcription and then for turning the transcript into concepts. The transcript is sent back to your device and stored there.

  • What is sent: the audio recording, and then its transcript. If the recap belongs to a book, the book's title and the page range are sent with it.
  • What happens to it: Readloop's service processes the request and returns the result. There is no database of anything you record, write or say, and no copy of your library on any server. The audio is held in memory for the length of the request and is not written to disk or to any storage bucket. The one thing the service does store is a short-lived request counter, described under the device identifier below, which never holds content.
  • Who else sees it: OpenAI, as the provider that performs the transcription and the text generation. Their handling of API data is governed by their own terms.
  • The recording itself stays on your device as well, so you can play it back or retry a failed transcription.

2. Your question and your own notes, when you use Ask

Ask answers questions about what you have already read. To do that, it sends your question along with the text of the notes it judged relevant, so the answer can be grounded in your own words.

  • What is sent: your question, plus excerpts from your own notes.
  • Ask does not search the web. Answers come from your notes and nothing else.
  • Same processing path as above: no stored copy of your question or your notes, and OpenAI as the provider.

3. Anonymous usage counts, which you can turn off

Readloop records which screens are opened and which buttons are tapped, so friction can be found and fixed. This goes to PostHog, hosted in the European Union.

These events never contain what you read, wrote, said or looked up. No book title, no author, no note text, no concept, no quote, no question, no word you searched for, and no file name. What travels is shapes rather than content: a count of pages, a count of characters, a duration in whole seconds, the name of a screen, and whether you have a subscription.

You can turn this off. Settings has a "Share usage data" switch. Turning it off stops the requests being made at all, rather than filtering them afterwards.

4. A bug report or an idea, only when you write one and tap Send

Settings has "Report a bug" and "Suggest an idea". Nothing is sent unless you type something and tap Send. This also goes to PostHog, in the same European Union project as above.

  • What is sent: the text you wrote. A bug report also carries the app version, the build number, your iOS version, and your two-letter region (for example FR), because a bug that cannot be reproduced cannot be fixed. An idea carries none of those, since none of them help.
  • You choose every word. This is the one thing here that is free text rather than a shape, so it contains whatever you put in it. If you would rather not include a book's title or a quote, leave it out and the report still works.
  • The "Share usage data" switch does not cover this, on purpose: turning off anonymous counting should not also take away your ability to report a problem. Sending is a tap you make each time.
  • Sent with the same random install identifier as the usage counts, so a follow-up report can be recognised as coming from the same install.

The device identifier, and your IP address

Because there is no account, Readloop's processing service identifies a caller by a random identifier generated once when you install the app. It is used to stop any single install from overwhelming the service. Deleting and reinstalling the app produces a new identifier.

Your IP address is also counted, for the same reason and nothing else. A random identifier can be changed by reinstalling, so on its own it cannot limit anyone determined to get around it. Every request to Readloop's service carries an IP address, as every request on the internet does.

Both are stored the same way: as a counter, keyed by the identifier or the IP together with which feature you used and the current hour, and nothing else. No content is stored with either one: no recording, no transcript, no note, no question, no book title.

  • The counter is held by Upstash, a hosted key-value service.
  • Each counter expires within an hour of being written, automatically, because the hour it belongs to is part of its name and a new hour writes a new one.
  • They are not linked together, and neither is used to build a profile, personalise anything, or recognise you between sessions.

Separately, Readloop's service writes a short server log line for each request, containing the name of the feature and how many bytes were sent. It carries no identifier and none of what you wrote or said. Those logs are kept by Vercel, which hosts the service, and expire on that service's own schedule.

Microphone and camera

  • Microphone, so you can speak your recap. Recording only happens when you tap the record button.
  • Camera, so you can scan a book's barcode or read a passage off the page. It runs only while that screen is open.
  • Photos: if you choose your own cover image, iOS hands Readloop only the single image you picked. The app never gets access to your photo library.

Subscriptions

Subscription purchases are handled by Apple, through RevenueCat as the service that tells the app whether a subscription is active. Readloop never sees your payment details.

Children

Readloop is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Deleting your data

Your library lives on your device, so deleting the app deletes it. Settings also has an option that erases everything the app has stored, including recordings, without deleting the app.

Because there is no account and no server-side copy of your library, there is nothing else to request deletion of. Usage counts, if you left them on, are tied to a random install identifier and nothing else: no name and no email address, because the app has never held either. As with every request on the internet, the connection itself carries an IP address, and Readloop asks the analytics service not to look up a location from it.

Changes
